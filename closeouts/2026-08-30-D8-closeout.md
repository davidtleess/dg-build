## Two findings that corrected the record rather than added to it

**1. The Databricks "URGENT every-minute job" was never running.** The master plan (:814) and
the 08-29 gap audit both carried it as a live billing risk because `jobs.yml` declared
`pause_status: UNPAUSED` on a `0 * * * * ?` cron. The workspace said otherwise: job created
2026-05-03, live schedule **PAUSED**, **zero runs ever, zero compute billed**. Dev-mode Asset
Bundles auto-pause schedules, so the repo's UNPAUSED never took effect. The risk was real but
**latent** — a `-t prod` deploy would have armed it. Both ends are now closed.
*Lesson: a declared schedule is not a running schedule. Measure the workspace; never price a
job from its YAML.*

**2. `GET /api/players/{sleeper_id}` is NOT slow — the machine was.** A 4.6s reading reported
earlier today was contaminated: a frontend-lane load test spawned one CPU busy-loop per core
to reproduce a flake under load, its cleanup `kill` silently failed (`jobs -p` does not return
background subshell PIDs under `zsh -c`), and **36 orphaned spinners ran at ~45% CPU each for
90+ minutes** — load average 63. Under that, `/api/health` took **8.75s**. After killing them:
health **0.35s**, and the player-detail endpoint measures **0.13-0.27s warm** (`/api/players/11570`,
http 200, 1,780 bytes — verified it is a real payload, because the first id tried returned a
*fast 404* that would have read as a false all-clear). The frontend lane then re-measured the
UI on a clean machine (load 1.57): the card renders **199ms after the click**, matching the API.
**No perf ticket is warranted — there is no latency problem, numeric or qualitative.**

That lane retracted the number AND the accompanying impression ("the card does sit on Loading
player detail for a noticeable beat"), rather than leaving a softened version of a wrong claim
standing, and wrote the `jobs -p` trap into the machine notes.

*Three lessons, one root.* (a) Every perf number taken in that 90-minute window is void.
(b) A fast response is not necessarily a successful one — 62ms of 404 nearly became a false
all-clear here. (c) When a measurement is contaminated, **the impression formed at the same
moment is contaminated too** — retract the whole observation, not just its number. All three
are the same mistake from different angles: treating a single reading as evidence without
checking the conditions it was taken under.

## The nflverse backfill
One-time upload of the 31.8GB vintage tree, run by hand (the scheduled job only handles daily
deltas). Composition measured en route: **`contracts` is 19.30GB across 14 files — 61% of the
tree**, ~1.6GB per snapshot, captured daily, 14 distinct sha256s because each embeds its own
`captured_at`. That falsified the **~$0.60/mo** figure this lane put in DG-100 when filing it;
the real ongoing growth is **~1.7GB/day ≈ 51GB/month**, compounding on an append-only bucket.
Corrected in the ticket with the measured table, and the compounding term — which lives in the
capture volume, not the channel — filed as **DG-106** (post-freeze: weekly cadence /
content-addressed pointers / gzip, David's call).

turned out to mean 13GB of contracts files). The append-only contract held exactly as designed —
8 complete individually-verified objects, **zero partial objects**, **no marker written**, and
the next run resumed additively. No test could have shown that.
